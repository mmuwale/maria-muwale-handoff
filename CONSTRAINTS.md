# Constraints

Rules that must survive the merge.

Each one is marked **CONTRACTUAL** (agreed with the candidate, or a matter of her
representation, and not mine or yours to change) or **TECHNICAL** (a decision made for
measurable reasons, reopenable with evidence). Both kinds are in force. The difference is
who can overrule them: contractual ones need Maria, technical ones need a number.

---

## 1. Real material only. No AI-generated visible assets. **CONTRACTUAL**

Nothing a visitor can see may be generated by an image model. Not the portrait, not the
crest, not a texture, not a background, not a placeholder.

Everything visible in this site comes from four sources:

- the campaign poster (`assets/poster.jpeg`), which is where the palette and the type pairing
  came from,
- two real studio photographs of the candidate,
- the real Strathmore University crest,
- hand-authored SVG geometry written as code.

**Hand-authored SVG geometry is code, not a generated asset.** The six stones in beat 5 are
literal `<path d="...">` strings in the markup. That is allowed. Feeding a prompt to an image
model is not.

**The reasoning.** This is a real candidate in a real election at a university that will
recognise its own crest. A generated asset that is spotted, or that redraws the crest
slightly wrong, is a credibility failure with no recovery inside the campaign window. There
is also no consent for it.

**This rule has been tested twice and held both times.** An image-model upscale of the crest
was proposed to sharpen it and was declined, because an image model redraws the lion, the
rose and the motto rather than sharpening them. And a replacement portrait cut-out was
verified pixel by pixel against the photograph already in the repository before it was used.

If you need a better crest, the only legitimate route is official artwork from Strathmore,
which their brand page says requires approval from `communications@strathmore.edu`.

## 2. Copy is her words, facts, or UI labels. Nothing invented. **CONTRACTUAL**

Every sentence on both pages is one of three things:

1. Maria's own wording, verbatim.
2. A verifiable fact (the election date, the polling hours, a contact address).
3. A UI label ("Scroll", "Share this page", "Read the manifesto").

There is no fourth category. No filler, no invented achievement, no "committed to
excellence" padding, no rewritten pillar that reads better.

**The manifesto may be re-arranged, never added to.** Reordering, splitting across pages,
changing emphasis, cutting: all fine. Adding a sentence she did not write: not fine, even if
it is obviously true and obviously helpful.

`CONTENT.md` holds the copy verbatim. Treat it as the source and the page as a rendering of
it.

**The reasoning.** She has to stand behind every word of this in front of people who know
her. Copy sign-off is closed. Reopening it costs her time in the last week of a campaign, so
the way to keep it closed is to not change the words.

## 3. Never an em dash. Never an en dash. Plain hyphen only. **TECHNICAL, and absolute**

Not U+2014 (em dash). Not U+2013 (en dash). A plain ASCII hyphen, `-`, everywhere.

This applies to:

- page copy,
- `<title>` and every meta tag,
- UI glyphs, including the countdown placeholders,
- these documents,
- **copy the client supplied that way.** Yes, even then. Convert it.

**The harness asserts zero of both on both pages, every run.** If you paste in copy from a
word processor, it will find it.

**The reasoning.** It is the single most recognisable tell of AI-authored text, and this is a
real candidate's site. It costs nothing to avoid and the credibility downside is total. Both
shipped pages are currently clean.

Note this is about the dashes only. Curly quotes and the middle dot `·` are used deliberately
and are fine.

## 4. Every page must be readable with JavaScript disabled. **TECHNICAL**

Both pages render every beat as readable static content with JS off, and again under
`prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`.

This is asserted structurally, not textually. The harness checks **rectangle containment and
pairwise bounding-box intersection**: is the element actually inside the visible box, and do
any two cards overlap. It does not ask whether the text exists.

**A text-presence check is not a visibility check.** This is the rule with the most scar
tissue behind it. Two separate bugs shipped in which content was present in `innerText` and
invisible to a reader, because it sat inside an `overflow:hidden` stage that only JavaScript
ever moved. Both the Record band and the beat-4 cards had this defect. `ARCHITECTURE.md`
sections 1 and 2 explain the mechanism.

**The reasoning.** Students on cheap phones, on throttled campus wifi, with data saver on,
with a script blocker, or on a connection that dropped the two vendored files. If the page
needs JavaScript to be legible, some fraction of the electorate reads nothing.

Known and accepted limitation: with JS off the beat-4 DOM order is four titles then four
wordings, interleaved visually by CSS `order`. `role="group"` and `aria-labelledby` tie each
wording to its pillar for screen readers.

## 5. Performance budget **TECHNICAL**

| | budget | now |
|---|---|---|
| First load, gzipped | **800 KB** | **173.0 KB**, about 22% |
| Hard ceiling | 1.5 MB | |
| JavaScript, gzipped | **100 KB** | **44.6 KB** |

Measured with `gzip -9` on the shipped files:

```
site/index.html                 raw 204,660   gz 131,505
site/vendor/gsap.min.js         raw  72,214   gz  28,012
site/vendor/ScrollTrigger.min.js raw 43,380   gz  17,663
                                          first load  177,180 B = 173.0 KB
```

`site/manifesto.html` is 14,469 B raw, 5,154 B gzipped, and is a separate navigation.

There is plenty of room. The point of stating the budget is not to defend the last kilobyte,
it is that **a merge that pulls in a framework, an icon font, a CSS library or a CDN script
changes the shape of this page, not just its size**. A 300 KB React bundle would still be
inside the budget and would still be the wrong answer, because it makes constraint 4
impossible.

**The reasoning.** Nairobi mobile data, campus wifi during a class change, and phones that
are not new. The measured target was always a page that opens fast on a bad connection.

## 6. The identity is locked **CONTRACTUAL**

Derived from the campaign poster in Phase 2 and unchanged since.

**Palette**

| token | value | use |
|---|---|---|
| `--ivory` | `#F6E9E0` | ground, beats 1 and 2 |
| `--navy` | `#0B1F3A` | ink on ivory; ground from beat 3 on |
| `--navyD` | `#071528` | manifesto only |
| `--blushW` | `#E09590` | **shapes only**, never type |
| `--blushI` | `#B26B67` | blush type on ivory |
| `--blushN` | `#D9A3A8` | blush type on navy |
| `--gold` | `#BFA671` | gold on navy |
| `--goldD` | `#A87B47` | gold on ivory |
| `#C1C1C0` | cool neutral grey | exactly one place: `h2.ph` on the manifesto |

**Gold is reserved.** Stones, rules, the sparkle, numerals, the countdown, the hashtag, and
the one write-on sweep. It is not a general accent colour. Spending it everywhere is what
makes it stop meaning anything.

**Type: Cormorant 600 caps for display, Montserrat 500 tracked for labels and 400 for body.**
Two families, no third.

**Fixed relationships:** MARIA is in the ground's ink, MUWALE is always blush. Ivory ground
for beats 1 and 2, navy from the foundation onward.

**The reasoning.** The poster is already circulating. A voter who has seen the poster and
then opens the site has to recognise it in under a second. That recognition is the whole job
of the identity, and it is why the palette is contractual rather than aesthetic.

## 7. Banned techniques **TECHNICAL**

Not on this page:

- WebGL and Three.js
- long scrubbed image sequences
- live SVG filters
- custom cursors
- magnetic hover
- **smooth-scroll libraries** (no Lenis, no locomotive)
- preloaders
- uniform fade-in-up applied to everything
- desktop-first structure with a degraded mobile version

**The reasoning is one sentence for most of them:** they cost battery and frames on a mid
phone and buy nothing a voter cares about.

Three deserve specifics:

- **Smooth-scroll libraries** hijack the scroll position, which breaks native
  `position:sticky`, breaks the browser's own scroll restoration, and would break the
  manifesto back link that restores a reader's exact position. The motion stack is locked at
  GSAP plus ScrollTrigger plus CSS sticky, and that is the whole of it.
- **Preloaders** trade a fast first paint for a slow one, on a page whose entire hero is
  already inlined so it can paint immediately.
- **Uniform fade-in-up on everything** is the default that makes a motion-designed page look
  like a template. Each beat here has its own motion; `.drop` and `.slide` are used
  deliberately and not everywhere.

Two further standing rules from the same list:

- **Desktop is designed per beat, never derived from mobile.** Look at the `@media
  (min-width:900px)` block: it is not a scale-up, it re-composes several beats (beat 3 becomes
  a two-column grid, beat 5 moves the type to the left and the stones to the right, beat 6
  becomes a two-column spread).
- **Motion is judged on live pages, never on stills, and scroll-linked means continuous.**
  If you change a beat, scroll it. A screenshot of the part you changed proves nothing; that
  is what the 16-cell pass is for.

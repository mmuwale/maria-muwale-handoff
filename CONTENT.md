## Provenance: three tiers, and they are not equal

**Tier 1: Maria's verbatim words.** Her four pillar wordings and the opening quote on the
manifesto. These came from blocks explicitly headed "Original manifesto wording" in the source
document she supplied. **Do not touch these at all**, including punctuation.

**Tier 2: client-supplied, condensed for the web.** The second paragraph under each manifesto
pillar (the `.web` class) and the manifesto coda. The source document was produced with AI
assistance, which the client disclosed. Only the tier-1 blocks in it are her own voice.
Everything else in it was expansion scaffolding, written in consultancy register, and it was
condensed hard for the web. Every date and every claim in the coda traces back to that
document; nothing was invented in the condensing.

**Tier 3: facts and UI labels.** The election date and hours, her record, contact handles, the
slogans, and interface words like "Scroll" and "Share this page".

When in doubt, treat it as tier 1.

## The opening quote

**Tier 1. Verbatim.**

> "I believe our academic experience should be just as engaging, connected and rewarding as
> our social life at Strathmore. As a class representative in both my previous and current
> years, and through my service in the 17th Academic Subcommittee, I have gained firsthand
> experience listening to students, working with representatives and understanding how student
> leadership works. I bring initiative, teamwork and a practical approach to leadership, and I
> am ready to turn ideas into meaningful action for students."

Section heading: **THE 4 PILLARS**

## The pillars, with their second paragraph

Each manifesto pillar carries **two** paragraphs. The first (`.said`) is her tier-1 wording
above, identical to beat 4. The second (`.web`) is tier 2:

**1. Smart Learning Spaces**

> Making it easier for students to discover suitable learning spaces and find their way around
> campus - by exploring a practical, university-approved pathway for better visibility of
> available spaces through existing systems.

**2. Interfaculty Projects**

> Starting with manageable pilots that bring students from different disciplines together to
> tackle real-world challenges and build solutions that no single field could create alone.

**3. Industrial Visits**

> Working with lecturers and relevant stakeholders to identify gaps, coordinate student demand
> and support meaningful exposure to workplaces and industries beyond the classroom.

**4. Faculty Spotlight Week**

> An academic discovery and collaboration week where students can discover projects, research,
> opportunities and expertise beyond their own timetables - and see how different disciplines
> connect.

## The coda

**Tier 2.** Every date here traces back to the source document.

> Each pillar begins with listening and one manageable pilot, not a university-wide launch. By
> the end of October 2026 all four have a first documented step; a feasibility decision on
> Smart Learning Spaces by the end of January 2027; the first interfaculty challenge and the
> first Faculty Spotlight Week by the end of April 2027. After each pilot or event, a short
> outcome summary is published.

And the limit, which is the most important paragraph on the page:

> These are proposed milestones, not promises that depend on approvals outside the Female
> academic Rep's control, and they assume a September 2026 start to the term. Not every idea
> can be delivered directly by one representative. The role is to represent, coordinate,
> advocate, follow up and help turn viable student priorities into action.

**Do not cut this paragraph for space.** It is what keeps every dated claim above it honest,
and it is the difference between a manifesto and an overpromise.
